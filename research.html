From interfaces to disease biology

Research

We study how brain barriers recognize and transport molecular cargo, and how these processes change in disease. We combine multivalent interface design, molecular probes, and imaging across scales to investigate barrier function, pathological molecule clearance, and responses to intervention.

01

Recognition & transport at brain barriers

The question

How do molecular interfaces influence receptor recognition, sorting, and transport?

Our approach

We investigate multivalent interactions and receptor-mediated transport, including LRP1, at the blood–brain and blood–brain tumor barriers (BBB/BBTB). Molecular design is connected to transport behavior, rather than treated as an end in itself.

02

Molecular tools to read biological change

The question

What can a molecular probe reveal about a cell state that an image alone cannot?

Our approach

Probe chemistry, fluorescence lifetime measurements, super-resolution microscopy, and multimodal imaging connect molecular interactions with cellular and tissue-level readouts. Each method is chosen for the biological question and the scale of evidence it can support.

03

Clearance pathways & disease response

The question

How are barrier function, pathological molecule clearance, and disease-associated cell states connected?

Our approach

Alzheimer’s disease provides a shared context for work on amyloid-β clearance, microglial lipid droplets, and the choroid plexus–cerebrospinal fluid pathway. Imaging and intervention studies are interpreted within their specific experimental or clinical setting.

Research in focus

Recognition & transport at brain barriers

From molecular recognition to amyloid-β clearance

The question

Can changing a multivalent interface alter how the brain barrier handles amyloid-β?

A transport-focused line of research connects receptor recognition and molecular design with pathological molecule clearance. The 2025 study reports enhanced Aβ clearance and cognitive outcomes in an APP/PS1 mouse model.

Evidence & context: Preclinical mouse evidence; it does not establish efficacy in patients.

Molecular tools to read biological change

Reading lipid-droplet states in microglia

The question

How do different Aβ25–35 assemblies relate to lipid-droplet remodeling and microglial function?

The polarity-sensitive probe BODIPY-LD reads lipid-droplet burden and microenvironment changes from cells to APP/PS1 brain tissue, linking lipid accumulation with reduced phagocytosis in BV2 cellular experiments.

Evidence & context: Cellular and mouse-tissue evidence; these readouts are not a validated clinical diagnostic test.

Clearance pathways & disease response

A barrier-and-clearance perspective on Alzheimer’s disease

The question

How is choroid plexus remodeling linked to CSF-mediated clearance and disease progression?

This study connects choroid plexus remodeling with impaired CSF-mediated clearance, adding a complementary perspective to blood–brain barrier transport research.

Evidence & context: A reported association does not by itself establish causality or treatment benefit.
